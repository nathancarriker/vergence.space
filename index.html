Identification

In 1944, this night was Uncle Rudy’s last. He’d have been asleep at Hardwick by now (or maybe not) but here I am, walking – yes, walking – the half-mile back to my hotel in the shadow of the Eiffel tower. On climbout tomorrow, I’ll nearly have a line of sight to where his plane was shot from the sky, eighty-two years ago.

My obsession, my drive to become a pilot overcame me early, seemingly from his ghost. But the poison of bullying also hit me early, and stayed. I barely remember any mirth of boyhood.

It all grew into, and became part of me. As with any toxin, I found a way to live with the poison. Flying has been the way – until the last piece stops moving.

But I watch them in the airports. I watch them on the sidewalks. People – nearly always in groups, with just the occasional lone straggler like me. Straggling like me, but not like me.

Not one like me.

Flying’s almost killed me a few times, but it saves me every day. It’s all I’ve had at times, and once, for one long year, I didn’t even have that.
